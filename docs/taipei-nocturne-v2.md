# Taipei nocturne, second homepage direction

User direction: remove numbered markers, arrows, oversized decorative serif type, and ornamental interface details. Make the homepage personal, considered, and artistic.

Afacad carries the homepage prose and navigation. Carter’s name uses Teko at weight 700, with a much larger scale and squared letterforms. On phones the name is deliberately stacked across two lines. Plain route links sit with the introduction.

Below the illustration, every essay uses its actual Substack cover, kept at its original proportions with the title and existing description underneath. Covers are stored locally as WebP assets and loaded through Next Image. The custom typographic jackets and their Barlow font are removed. Native horizontal scrolling supports touch, a trackpad, and keyboard focus. Source mappings are recorded in `docs/substack-covers.md`.

Projects appear as larger, unframed screenshots with simple captions: two columns on desktop and one on phones. Both collections read directly from `content/portfolio.ts` and link to their full archives; no title is singled out as a favorite. Titles remain visible without hover, and reduced-motion preferences disable the small hover movements. The shared cursor remains unchanged.

Artwork: `public/taipei-nocturne-v2.png`, generated with the built-in image tool. The original first-pass artwork remains in version history and is not loaded by this page.

## Artwork prompt

Use case: stylized-concept.
Create a finished original nocturne illustration for Carter Wang's personal website. Wide landscape 16:9 composition, suitable for 1600px display. Artwork only, no words.
Art direction: a confident, quiet painting made with matte gouache, chalk pastel and dry brush on dark blue board. Visible broad economical brush marks, subtle irregular edges, gently imperfect geometry, large intentional flat shapes. It should feel like an illustration commissioned for a thoughtful independent book, not a 3D render, not fantasy concept art, not a video game, not photorealistic.
Scene: an intimate Taipei rooftop at night, overlooking a distant skyline with Taipei 101. At the foreground LEFT, a small writing desk with one amber lamp and open pages; center is a worn muted burnt-orange basketball half-court with a single hoop; distant city is on the RIGHT. These belong to one continuous lived-in rooftop. A simple dark railing and a few quiet plant silhouettes, nothing overly detailed. Very subtle celestial suggestion: sparse stars, a deep-blue soft atmospheric arc far behind the skyline. Night as a place to think. No spectacular galaxy, no sci-fi props, no giant glowing planet.
Composition: rooftop and desk occupy lower 55%, city rises in right third. Top-left 55% is unbroken dark blue negative space. Desk and court are simple intelligible forms; render only the details necessary to recognize them. The entire composition is asymmetrical, beautifully balanced. The lamp is the smallest and warmest point of light.
Palette: inky blue-black #111c2b, chalky deep ultramarine, smoky teal, restrained oxidized vermilion on court, a very few dull amber lights. Avoid cream-colored sky, candy pastels, bright neon, bloom and glossy lighting. Matte pigments, areas of dry texture, calm silhouettes.
No typography, no lettering, no numbers, no labels, no symbols, no borders, no UI, no watermarks, no people. Make the restraint, brushwork, color relationships, and negative space exceptional.
